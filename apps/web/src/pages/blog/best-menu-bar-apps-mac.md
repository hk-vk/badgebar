The best Mac utilities stay out of your way. They live in the menu bar, show you one useful thing
at a glance, and open with a click. These are ten worth installing, grouped by what they do. Most
are free, and many are open source.

## Keep the menu bar tidy

### Thaw, Ice or Hidden Bar

Once you install a few of the apps below, you'll want a way to hide the icons you rarely need.
[Thaw](https://github.com/thaw-app/Thaw) (macOS 26 or later) and its parent
[Ice](https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice) hide icons behind a divider and can show them in a
separate bar, which helps on a MacBook with a notch.
[Hidden Bar](https://github.com/dwarvesf/hidden) does the same with a single arrow. All three are
free and open source. For a full comparison, see
[free Bartender alternatives](/blog/free-bartender-alternatives/).

## See what's new

### BadgeBar

If you hide the Dock, you lose the red unread badges on its icons. [BadgeBar](/) puts them in the
menu bar instead: each app gets its own icon with its count on top, like Slack 12, Mail 3,
WhatsApp 2. Click an icon to open the app, or right-click it to read the latest messages. Free and
open source.

### MeetingBar

[MeetingBar](https://github.com/leits/MeetingBar) shows your next calendar event in the menu bar
and gives you a one-click button to join the call, whether it's on Zoom, Google Meet or Teams.
Free and open source.

### Itsycal

[Itsycal](https://www.mowglii.com/itsycal/) is a small calendar that opens from the menu bar, with
your upcoming events underneath. It's a quick replacement for opening the Calendar app just to
check a date. Free.

## Watch your Mac

### Stats

[Stats](https://github.com/exelban/stats) shows CPU, memory, disk, network, battery and sensor
readings in the menu bar, each as its own small widget you can turn on or off. Free and open
source.

### iStat Menus

[iStat Menus](https://bjango.com/mac/istatmenus/) covers the same ground as Stats with more detail,
history graphs and polish. Paid, with a free trial.

### AlDente

[AlDente](https://apphousekitchen.com) limits how far your MacBook battery charges, which can help
it last longer if your laptop stays plugged in. The core feature is free, with a Pro version for
more control.

## Work faster

### Maccy

[Maccy](https://maccy.app) keeps a history of everything you copy. Press its shortcut, type to
search, and paste something you copied an hour ago. Free and open source.

### Rectangle

[Rectangle](https://rectangleapp.com) snaps windows to halves, thirds and corners with keyboard
shortcuts or by dragging them to the edge of the screen. Free and open source.

### Amphetamine

[Amphetamine](https://apps.apple.com/app/amphetamine/id937984704) keeps your Mac awake for as long
as you choose, or while a particular app is running or a download finishes. Free on the Mac App
Store.

## Putting it together

A setup many people settle on: hide the Dock, keep the menu bar for things you check often, and
tuck everything else behind Thaw or Hidden Bar. With [BadgeBar](/) showing unread counts and
MeetingBar showing your next call, there's little reason to bring the Dock back. See
[how to hide the Dock on Mac](/blog/hide-dock-mac/) to get started.
